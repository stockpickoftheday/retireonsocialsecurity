Where to retire · Making your check go further

Best Places to Retire in the US — and the Cheapest Countries to Retire Abroad

Once you know your monthly Social Security check, the next question is where it goes furthest. The best places to retire in the US are not the famous Florida beach towns this year — they are small, affordable cities where the average benefit of about $2,071 a month covers the rent with room to spare. And if you want your dollars to stretch further still, the cheapest countries to retire abroad offer a comfortable life for $1,000 to $2,500 a month. Both lists below come from published 2026 rankings, and the source of every figure is named on this page.

Top 5 retirement destinations in the US

From U.S. News & World Report's 2026 Best Places to Retire, which ranked more than 850 cities on quality of life, affordability, health care, retiree taxes, job market, and senior migration. The cost-of-living index is 100 for the US average — lower means cheaper. Home values and rents are from Homes.com's write-up of the same ranking.

In The Woodlands it does not. Run your own benefit on the Social Security calculator and compare it with the rent where you want to live.

A couple with two benefits has far more room. But budgets are only half the story — visas, health care, and distance from family matter just as much.

Getting paid abroad, and health care

Social Security can usually still be paid to you abroad. The Social Security Administration has a Payments Abroad Screening Tool to check whether your country is covered — most are, with a few exceptions. But Medicare generally does not cover care outside the US, so you would need local health insurance or pay out of pocket. The standard Part B premium was $202.90 a month in 2026 and still applies if you keep your Medicare coverage while living abroad.
