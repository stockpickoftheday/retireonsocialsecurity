Taxes · Federal tax on benefits

Is Social Security Taxable?

Yes, it can be. Whether you owe tax depends on your combined income: your adjusted gross income, plus half your Social Security, plus any tax-free interest. For single filers, if combined income is under $25,000 ($32,000 for couples), none of it is taxed. Between $25,000 and $34,000 single ($32,000 to $44,000 joint), up to 50% can be taxed. Above that, up to 85% can be taxed. Nine states also tax benefits to some degree. Most people with only Social Security and little other income pay no tax on it at all.

What is "combined income"?

The IRS adds three things: your adjusted gross income (everything except Social Security), plus half of your Social Security benefits, plus any tax-free interest (for example, from municipal bonds). That total is your combined income. It is the only number that decides whether your benefits are taxed.

These thresholds have never been adjusted for inflation — they are the same numbers Congress set in 1983 and 1993. As benefits rise with cost-of-living adjustments, more people cross them each year.

An example

A single retiree gets $24,000 a year from Social Security and has $10,000 of other income. Combined income is $10,000 + $12,000 (half the benefit) = $22,000 — under $25,000, so none of the benefit is taxed. Give that same person $20,000 of other income instead, and combined income is $32,000 — between $25,000 and $34,000, so up to 50% of the benefit can be taxed, worked out on the IRS worksheet (Publication 915).

"Up to 85% taxable" is not an 85% tax rate

It means up to 85% of your benefit is added to your taxable income, and then taxed at your normal income tax rate. At most 85% of your benefit can ever be counted — at least 15% is always tax-free federally.

State taxes

Nine states tax Social Security benefits to some degree. The rest do not tax them at all. State rules change often and many states exempt lower incomes, so check your own state's rules before you count on the federal answer alone.

Can you have tax withheld?

Yes. You can ask Social Security to withhold federal tax from your check at 7%, 10%, 12%, or 22% by filing Form W-4V. It is a simple way to avoid a surprise bill at tax time. Your SSA-1099, which arrives each January, shows how much you received and is what you use on your tax return.

This page is general information, not tax advice. The exact taxable amount comes from the IRS worksheet in Publication 915. If your income is near a threshold, a tax preparer can usually save you more than they cost.
