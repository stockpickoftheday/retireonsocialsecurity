Claiming age · The biggest decision in Social Security

Should I Take Social Security at 62 or 70?

There's no one right answer. It's a bet on how long you'll live. Take it at 62 and you get a smaller check, 70% of your full benefit, but for eight more years. Wait until 70 and the check is 77% bigger for life, but you skip eight years of payments. The two roughly even out around age 80 or 81: live past that and waiting wins; don't, and claiming early was right. Health, family history, whether you need the money now, and whether you're married all tilt the answer.

When to take Social Security

You can start Social Security any time from 62 to 70, and the month you pick is permanent. Full retirement age is 67 for anyone born in 1960 or later. Claim before that and your check is cut, by 30% at 62. Wait past it and it grows by 8% a year until 70, when it stops growing. There's no benefit to waiting past 70. Most people decide based on three things: how long they expect to live, whether they need the money now, and whether they're still working. Earning too much before full retirement age can mean some benefits are withheld.

How much Social Security will I get at 62?

At 62 you get 70% of your full benefit, permanently. If your full benefit at 67 would be $2,000 a month, claiming at 62 gives you about $1,400. The average retired worker gets about $2,071 a month at full retirement age in 2026, so the average 62-year-old claimer gets roughly $1,450. The reduction never goes away. You get the smaller amount for the rest of your life, adjusted only for cost-of-living increases. But you also get five extra years of checks before someone waiting until 67 gets anything.

The case for claiming at 62

  • You need the money now. If you have stopped working and have no other income, the check is there for a reason.
  • Your health is poor, or your parents died young. If you do not expect to live past about 80, claiming early usually pays more in total.
  • You want to stop working. Social Security was built to replace wages. If work is done, take the check.

The case for waiting until 70

  • You expect a long life. Past about 80 or 81, the bigger check has paid back everything you skipped, and every month after that is pure gain.
  • You are the higher earner in a couple. When you die, your spouse steps into your check. A bigger check protects them for the rest of their life. See spousal and survivor benefits.
  • You are still working. Waiting means no earnings test, and your later earnings can even raise your benefit if they replace a low year in your 35-year average.

The case for 67, the middle road

Claiming at full retirement age is the compromise: no cut, no eight-year wait, and no earnings limit from that month on. For many people it is the sensible default, especially if you retire right around 67 anyway.

Working while you collect

Before full retirement age, Social Security withholds $1 for every $2 you earn above $24,480 in 2026. In the year you reach full retirement age, the limit is $65,160 and the withholding is $1 for every $3, counting only earnings before the month you reach it. Once you reach full retirement age there is no limit at all. The withheld money is not lost — your benefit is recalculated upward once you reach full retirement age.

There is no way to know how long you will live, so there is no way to be sure which age was best until afterward. Run your own numbers on the calculator, and look at the break-even ages before you decide. This page is education, not financial advice.

Common questions

Is it better to take Social Security at 62 or 70?

There is no one right answer — it is a bet on how long you will live. Live past about 80 or 81 and waiting wins; if not, claiming early was the right call.

How much less do I get at 62?

30% less than your full benefit, permanently — 70% of what you would get at 67.

How much more do I get at 70?

24% more than your full benefit — 124% of it — for life.

Can I still work if I claim early?

Yes, but before full retirement age Social Security withholds $1 for every $2 you earn above $24,480 in 2026. Once you reach full retirement age there is no limit.
