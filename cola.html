Social Security COLA: How Cost-of-Living Adjustments Work

Every year, Social Security raises benefits to keep up with inflation. The increase is called a cost-of-living adjustment, or COLA. It is automatic — Congress does not vote on it — and it is based on a price index called the CPI-W, which tracks what urban wage earners actually buy. The COLA for 2026 is 2.8%, and for 2027 it is 3.6%. Once your benefit starts, every COLA raises it for life, so the adjustments compound year after year.

How the COLA is set

Each October, the Social Security Administration compares the average CPI-W for July through September with the same three months a year earlier. If prices rose, benefits rise by the same percentage starting with December benefits, paid in January. If prices did not rise, there is no COLA that year — benefits never go down.

What the COLA does — and does not — do

  • It protects your buying power. A $2,000 check in 2026 becomes $2,056 after a 2.8% COLA, and the next year's COLA applies to the new, larger amount.
  • It applies whether or not you have claimed yet. Your benefit amount grows with COLAs from age 62 onward, even if you wait until 70 to claim.
  • It does not track your costs exactly. The CPI-W is based on working people's spending, not retirees'. Health care, which retirees buy more of, has often risen faster — which is why many retirees feel the COLA falls short.
  • Medicare premiums can eat it. The standard Part B premium is usually deducted from your check, and when it rises, it takes a bite out of the COLA. A "hold harmless" rule limits how much of the increase most people can lose in a year.

The calculator on this site gives estimates in today's dollars — it does not add future COLAs, because nobody knows what they will be. Your real check will be higher than the estimate if you claim years from now, because COLAs will have raised it in the meantime. Figures verified November 2026.

Common questions

What is the Social Security COLA?

The yearly cost-of-living adjustment that raises benefits with inflation. It is automatic and based on a price index called the CPI-W.

What is the COLA for 2026 and 2027?

2.8% in 2026 and 3.6% in 2027.

Does the COLA apply before I claim?

Yes. Your benefit amount grows with COLAs from age 62 onward, even if you wait until 70 to claim.

Can the COLA ever be zero?

Yes. If prices did not rise, there is no COLA that year — but benefits never go down.
